SEC Derivatives Weekly

24–31 July 2026

Intel’s escrowed-shares derivative blew up to a $16 billion liability, Microsoft quietly assembled a $64 billion FX book, and Darden Restaurants tore up its hedging playbook entirely. Meanwhile Halliburton is rapidly unwinding a rare corporate CDS portfolio. Five clear leads below.

Story Leads

Intel’s derivative liability hits $16.2B on escrowed-shares instrument. INTC’s total derivative liability surged 228% quarter-on-quarter, driven almost entirely by the Escrowed Shares derivative (tied to the Ireland SCIP arrangement) swinging to a $15.6B liability from $2.7B at year-end. That generated a $12.5B loss in Q2 alone — extraordinary for a non-financial. The instrument has been wildly volatile since first appearing in Sep 2025 ($7.1B liability) and is now dwarfing everything else on Intel’s derivatives book.

Microsoft’s non-designated FX notional jumps 352% to $63.5B. The 10-K reveals MSFT has nearly quintupled its non-designated FX derivatives in a year while keeping its hedge-accounted book flat at ~$1.5B. The company now runs a $65B FX book, almost all economic hedges without designation. Total derivative assets doubled to $2.2B. Worth asking: what drove the shift away from hedge accounting, and how much P&L volatility is this creating?

Darden Restaurants exits FX entirely, pivots to IR swaps and equity derivatives. DRI’s 10-K shows a complete restructuring: the $815M FX forward programme in place for years dropped to zero, replaced by $300M of IR swaps (carrying $36M negative fair value), $83M in equity derivatives, and a small commodity position. A restaurant chain voluntarily unwinding a large FX hedge book and pivoting to rate risk is an unusual move — the swap loss suggests rates moved against them after inception.

Halliburton winds down its CDS book by 71% in 18 months. HAL’s CDS notional — credit protection on Mexican customer borrowings — fell from $739M (Dec 2024) to $217M (Jun 2026). Non-financial S&P 500 companies using credit default swaps are rare; the steady wind-down raises questions about whether Pemex-related credit exposure is genuinely improving or Halliburton is simply accepting the risk unhedged.

PPG Industries ramps FX derivatives tenfold to $3.8B. The coatings maker went from $375M in total FX/IR notional at year-end to $3.8B by Q2, adding $3.4B in non-designated FX forwards in two quarters. The net derivative position flipped from liability to asset. PPG appears to be aggressively scaling up balance-sheet hedging of its international revenues.
