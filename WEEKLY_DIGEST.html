Story Leads

Constellation Brands reveals $3.3B FX book — hedges overwhelmingly in the money. STZ’s Q1 FY2027 10-Q is the first quarter to break out full notional and fair value data after quarters of reporting only hedge ratios. The numbers: $3.3B in FX forwards, $369M in commodity derivatives, and a net derivative asset of $361M ($377M assets vs. $16M liabilities). The company has 84% of FX and 89% of commodity exposures hedged. That net asset figure is striking — the entire hedging programme is deeply profitable at current rates.

Micron’s non-designated FX hedges nearly double to $12.4B. MU’s Q3 10-Q shows non-designated FX forwards surging 79% quarter-on-quarter to $12.4B, pushing total FX notional to $22.1B. This is one of the largest corporate FX programmes in the S&P 500 — now bigger than many pharma multinationals. Worth asking whether HBM capacity build-out in Asia or the DRAM pricing cycle is driving the expansion.

General Mills’ 10-K reveals a $227M equity derivatives position. GIS’s FY2026 annual filing shows the expected multinational food hedging book — $1.4B FX, $1.6B IR swaps — plus $227M in equity derivatives. That’s unusual for a consumer staples company. The notes should clarify whether this hedges executive comp, pension obligations, or strategic investments. Net derivative position is a modest +$15M.

McCormick running nearly $2B in FX forwards for a spice company. MKC’s Q2 10-Q shows $1.95B in FX forwards (97% designated), $500M IR swaps, and $302M commodity derivatives. With $49M expected to reclassify from AOCI in the next 12 months, the hedging programme has meaningful P&L impact. A useful data point for covering mid-cap corporates with sophisticated hedging infrastructure.

Kroger activates; cash flow hedge AOCI flips negative. KR entered the monitored universe this week via its FY2026 10-K. Cash flow hedge AOCI swung from +$48M to −$8M year-on-year. Variable-rate debt dropped 41% to $88M, and commodity derivatives shrank from $32M to $26M. The grocer carries $15.9B in fixed-rate debt against $14.9B fair value — still above water, but sensitive to where rates go next.
