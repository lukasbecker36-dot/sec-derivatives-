Story Leads

GE Aerospace launches $1.15B interest rate swap programme. The Q2 2026 10-Q shows GE Aerospace entered $1.15B in qualifying IR swaps — up from zero at year-end 2025. Total FX derivatives notional stands at $9.3B ($4.9B designated, $4.4B non-designated), and the company reported $113M in derivative assets against $132M in liabilities. Meanwhile, commodity derivatives ($145M last quarter) vanished from the filing entirely. Post-spin from GE Vernova, this looks like GE Aerospace building out a financial-risk management book befitting a pure-play aerospace company. Worth asking whether the IR swaps are fixing floating-rate debt or managing pension-related duration.

ConAgra nearly halves commodity hedging. The FY2026 10-K (period ending May 2026) shows commodity derivatives notional at $77.6M, down 44.6% from $140.2M a year ago. Energy commodity sensitivity has also been trending lower — from $5.7M two years ago to $3.8M now. FX derivatives notional came in at $95.2M. For a packaged-food company built on agricultural and energy inputs, cutting commodity hedge coverage by almost half is a notable shift. Lower input-cost expectations, or a move to supplier-contract pass-throughs?

Nike’s FX value-at-risk drops 38%. Nike’s FY2026 10-K shows FX VaR fell from $107M to $66M, attributed to lower foreign currency volatilities. The $2.4B IR swap book is unchanged, and long-term debt sits at $8B principal ($6.8B fair value). Nike uses VaR rather than the standard 10% hypothetical sensitivity, which makes cross-company comparisons tricky — but the direction is clear and the magnitude is significant.

PepsiCo update: cross-currency swaps unwind, Celsius losses mount. Following up on last week’s initial PEP extraction: the $700M in maturing cross-currency swaps (programme now $1.0B, down from $1.7B) and $371M in unrealized losses on the Celsius stake ($1.45B, Level 3) remain the key stories. The Poppi contingent consideration at $117M (down $161M) also bears watching for potential milestone write-downs.
