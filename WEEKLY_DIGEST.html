SEC Derivatives Weekly

14–21 August 2026

A quiet week by filing volume — the pipeline processed six filings across five issuers (Amcor, Western Digital, Analog Devices, Estée Lauder, Applied Materials, Nordson). Two of the six — Western Digital’s and Estée Lauder’s new 10-Ks — came through as largely blank extractions, so there isn’t much to say about either yet. The clearest verified move is Analog Devices, where the FX hedge book grew sharply and the derivative liability position roughly doubled.

Story Leads

Analog Devices’ FX notional book expands 44%, derivative liability position jumps 56%. ADI’s total FX derivatives notional rose from $662.2M (quarter ended May 2, 2026) to $950.3M (quarter ended August 1, 2026), +43.5%. Both sides of the book grew in step: the designated portion rose from $343.2M to $495.2M (+44.3%) and the non-designated portion from $319.0M to $455.1M (+42.7%). Over the same quarter, total derivative liability rose from $33.9M to $53.0M (+56.4%), and net derivative position worsened from −$27.4M to −$45.8M (−67.0%). The pipeline’s own automated flag attributes the liability jump to an interest-rate swap fair-value-hedge loss, but the underlying filing text captured in notes.txt is truncated before the dollar figures for that loss appear, so that specific driver isn’t independently verified here (see Worth Asking). Also worth noting: five previously-populated fair-value-note fields (cash equivalents, marketable securities, corporate debt securities, and the fair-value-note derivative asset/liability totals) went blank in this extraction — a partial gap alongside the confirmed derivatives-note figures above.

Applied Materials: new $500M interest-rate swap notional, FX sensitivity widens 41%. AMAT’s IR swap notional rose from $400M (quarter ended April 26, 2026) to $500M (quarter ended July 26, 2026), +25.0%. The pipeline flags a caveat worth carrying into any story: the $500M figure is described in the filing as swaps entered into during the nine-month period, not necessarily the total outstanding at quarter-end, so it may not be a clean like-for-like with the prior $400M figure — worth checking against the primary filing before citing as a straight period-over-period increase. Separately, AMAT’s 10% FX sensitivity scenario widened from −$213M to −$301M (−41.3%), a comparison between two like-for-like fields. Five fair-value-note fields (cash equivalents, marketable securities, marketable equity securities, corporate debt securities, unrealized gains) went blank this quarter; the filing text is flagged as cut off mid-sentence before the relevant summary table.

Nordson: derivative asset fair value down 36%, hedge adjustment on debt down 86%. NDSN’s total derivative asset fell from $18.4M (quarter ended April 30, 2026) to $11.7M (quarter ended July 31, 2026), −36.5%. Its cumulative fair-value-hedge adjustment to carrying debt fell from $6.4M to $0.9M over the same quarter, −86.2%. Six fields that break out the notional by instrument type (FX, cross-currency swap, interest-rate swap) went blank this quarter, though the aggregate notional and fair-value fields above did extract.

Amcor: fair-value-note derivative asset down 38%, on a small base. AMCR’s total derivative asset fell from $13M (quarter ended March 31, 2026) to $8M (fiscal year ended June 30, 2026), −38.5%. Total derivative liability was roughly flat, $467M to $471M. These are small-dollar fair-value marks relative to the company’s ~$3.7B FX notional book (essentially unchanged quarter to quarter, $3,749M to $3,700M), so the percentage move is real but the underlying figures are minor.
