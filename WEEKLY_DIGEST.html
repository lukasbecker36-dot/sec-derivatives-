Story Leads

Eli Lilly’s non-designated FX book surges to $28B. LLY’s total FX derivatives notional jumped from $19,114M (Q1 2026) to $28,238M (Q2 2026), a $9.1B quarter-on-quarter increase. Nearly all of it sits in the non-designated book, which grew from $18,817M to $27,992M; the designated portion edged down from $297M to $246M. Lilly also carries $3.6B in foreign-currency notes designated as net investment hedges (per the Q2 filing text). The Q2 extraction was partial — fair value data dropped out — so derivative asset/liability comparisons are unavailable. Worth asking: what is driving the scale-up of a non-designated FX programme that is now roughly 100x the size of the designated one?

Disney’s net derivative position deteriorates by $724M on flat notionals. DIS reported a net derivative position of −$779M at June 27, 2026, down from −$55M at March 28 (fiscal Q2 → Q3 FY2026). Derivative liabilities rose from $1,381M to $1,689M while assets dipped from $777M to $753M. Yet notionals barely moved: FX forwards at $14,200M (from $14,600M), IR swaps at $12.0B (from $12.1B). The swing is almost entirely mark-to-market. Meanwhile, the expected 12-month AOCI reclassification reversed from a −$56M loss to a +$14M gain — suggesting the pain is concentrated in fair-value-hedged instruments.

Occidental’s crude collars flip from $465M net liability to $227M net asset. OXY’s net derivative position swung from −$465M (Q1 2026) to +$227M (Q2 2026). Derivative liabilities collapsed from $649M to $65M (−90%), while assets grew from $184M to $292M. The filing identifies two-way crude oil collars entered in February 2026 (notional: 100 Mbbl/day, floor WTI $55/bbl, weighted-average ceiling $75.89/bbl) as the key instrument. The Q1 collar liability of $292M has largely unwound.

Zimmer Biomet adds a $1.25B non-designated FX tranche — a structural shift. ZBH’s FX derivatives notional jumped from $2,095M to $3,455M (Q1 → Q2 2026, +65%). The company had been 100% designated for FX hedging; it now carries a new $1,250M non-designated forward book alongside $2,205M in designated hedges. Worth asking: is Zimmer layering on balance-sheet hedges for a specific currency or acquisition-related exposure?

FedEx’s FY2026 10-K reveals a new $2.3B interest rate swap programme. FDX’s annual report (fiscal year ended May 31, 2026) discloses $2,300M of IR swaps not designated as hedges, up from zero in the prior year. FX derivatives also grew: non-designated notional increased from $1,118M (FY2025) to $1,753M, with $1,000M in newly designated FX hedges. Total net derivative position: −$94M. The FX sensitivity figure widened from −$450M (FY2025 10-K) to −$541M (FY2026 10-K) for a 10% adverse move’s full-year operating income impact.

Albemarle launches a $1.1B designated FX hedge programme. ALB’s FX derivatives notional rose from $1,600M (Q1 2026, all non-designated) to $2,500M (Q2 2026), with $1,100M now designated as hedges and $1,400M non-designated. This is a structural shift: Albemarle had previously run its entire FX book as economic hedges without designation. The Q2 alert log identifies a new EUR 1 billion cross-currency swap designated as a net investment hedge of EUR-functional-currency subsidiaries.
