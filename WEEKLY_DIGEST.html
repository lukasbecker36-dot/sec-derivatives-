Story Leads

PepsiCo reveals $7.5B derivatives portfolio across three asset classes. PEP’s fiscal Q3 10-Q (period ending 13 June, processed 10 July) is the pipeline’s first successful extraction for the company. The numbers: $3.9B in FX derivatives (combining $2.9B in currency forwards with $1.0B in cross-currency swaps), $2.0B in interest-rate swaps, and $1.6B in commodity hedges. Net derivative position is +$51M ($207M assets, $156M liabilities). The cross-currency swap book shrank from $1.7B to $1.0B as $700M in contracts matured — with $300M in new Thai baht swaps entered post-quarter.

PepsiCo designated $7.4B of euro debt as net investment hedges. During the 24-week period, PEP designated $4.5B of existing euro-denominated debt plus $2.9B of newly issued euro debt as net investment hedges. These are non-derivative instruments, so they don’t appear in the derivative notionals, but they represent a major balance-sheet hedging decision. Worth asking what’s driving the ramp-up in European net-asset protection.

PepsiCo’s Celsius bet: $1.5B position with $371M in paper losses. The same filing shows PEP’s Celsius Holdings available-for-sale debt security at $1,453M fair value (Level 3), carrying $371M in unrealized losses over 24 weeks. Meanwhile, the Poppi (VNGR Beverage) contingent consideration liability fell $161M to $117M, potentially signalling a milestone shortfall on that acquisition. Two energy-drink investment stories in one note — one losing money, one with deal targets slipping.

Pipeline gap: no PepsiCo history to compare against. PEP’s five earlier filing periods (Q1 2025 through Q2 2026) all hit API authentication errors during a previous extraction run. Only this latest quarter succeeded, via Claude Code mode. That means we can’t yet show how the hedging book has evolved — but it establishes a baseline for tracking from here.
