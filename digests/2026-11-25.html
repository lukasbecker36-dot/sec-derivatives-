Leads

SJM (The J.M. Smucker Company), 10-Q for the period ended October 31, 2026 — the filing discloses the termination of interest-rate contracts tied to its Senior Notes: "The reclassification includes terminated contracts which were designated as cash flow hedges." The company reports deferred net pre-tax losses of $98.6 million in AOCI as of October 31, 2026, versus $104.9 million at April 30, 2026 (per the filing's own comparison), and notes "Approximately $12.5 of the net pre-tax loss will be recognized over the next 12 months related to the terminated interest rate contracts." Separately, FX derivative notional not designated as hedges fell to $47.1 million from $74.2 million (prior period ended July 31, 2026), a 36.5% decline.
BDX (Becton, Dickinson and Company), 10-K for the period ended September 30, 2026 — the filing flags a new-instruments disclosure on hedging its investments in foreign subsidiaries: "the Company has hedged the currency risk associated with those investments with certain instruments such as foreign currency-denominated debt and cross-currency swaps, which are designated …" Total FX derivative notional rose to $8,506 million from $6,584 million (prior period ended June 30, 2026, +29.2%), driven largely by non-designated notional, which climbed to $6,116 million from $3,996 million (+53.1%).
ADI (Analog Devices), 10-K for the period ended October 31, 2026 — interest-rate swap notional nearly tripled year over year: "As of October 31, 2026 we had $3.0 billion notional of fixed for floating interest rate swaps outstanding, with the swaps having a combined net payable fair value of $42.3 million," up from $1.0 billion notional and a $12.6 million fair value at November 1, 2025. The filing adds that "a hypothetical 100-basis point increase in interest rates would increase the combined swap payable balance by approximately $163.2 million," up from $45.9 million a year earlier. Outstanding senior notes also grew: "As of October 31, 2026, we had $11.2 billion in principal amount of senior unsecured notes outstanding, with a fair value of $10.2 billion," versus $8.2 billion a year earlier.
WDAY (Workday), 10-Q for the period ended October 31, 2026 — the filing reiterates its foreign-currency hedging programme, noting it "enter[s] into foreign currency forward contracts to hedge a portion of our forecasted revenue and expense transactions ('cash flow hedges')," with resulting gains and losses booked to AOCI and reclassified into earnings as the hedged transactions settle. The filing's notes do not explain this quarter's derivative-asset and net-position movements — see the supplementary table below.
