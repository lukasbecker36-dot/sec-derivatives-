--- 10-K | Period ending 2025-12-31 ---

  [FX forwards / hedging policy]
    - NOTE 9 – FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS Currency Hedging Instruments The gross notional amount of the Company’s currency derivatives was $ 320.1 million and $ 167.2 million at December 31, 2025 and 2024, respectively.
    - Concentration of Credit Risk The counterparties to the Company’s forward contracts consist of a number of investment grade major international financial institutions.
    - We attempt to hedge exposures that cannot be naturally offset to an insignificant amount with foreign currency derivatives.

  [FX exposure]
    - QUANTITATIVE AND QUALITATIVE DISCLOSURES ABOUT MARKET RISK We are exposed to fluctuations in currency exchange rates, commodity prices and interest rates which could impact our results of operations and financial condition.
    - As a result, we are exposed to movements in exchange rates of various currencies against the U.S. dollar as well as against other currencies throughout the world.
    - We evaluate our exposure to changes in currency exchange rates on our foreign currency derivatives using a sensitivity analysis.
    - The sensitivity analysis is a measurement of the potential loss in fair value based on a percentage change in exchange rates.
    - Based on the firmly committed currency derivative instruments in place at December 31, 2025, a hypothetical change in fair value of those derivative instruments assuming a 10% adverse change in exchange rates would result in an additional unrealized loss of approximately $4.3 million.

  [Debt / notes issuance]
    - Interest Rate Exposure Of our total outstanding indebtedness of $2.0 billion as of December 31, 2025, approximately 90% incurs fixed-rate interest and is therefore not exposed to the risk of rising variable interest rates.

  [Interest rate risk]
    - QUANTITATIVE AND QUALITATIVE DISCLOSURES ABOUT MARKET RISK We are exposed to fluctuations in currency exchange rates, commodity prices and interest rates which could impact our results of operations and financial condition.
    - Interest Rate Exposure Of our total outstanding indebtedness of $2.0 billion as of December 31, 2025, approximately 90% incurs fixed-rate interest and is therefore not exposed to the risk of rising variable interest rates.
    - At December 31, 2025, the outstanding borrowings of $190.6 million under the Revolving Facility accrue interest at SOFR plus a margin of 1.125%, resulting in an interest rate of 4.902%.
    - We are also exposed to the risk of rising interest rates to the extent that we fund our operations with short-term or variable-rate borrowings.
    - A hypothetical increase of 1% in the interest rate on the variable rate borrowings under our Revolving Facility would increase our interest expense over the next twelve months by $2.0 million based on the balances outstanding as of December 31, 2025.

  [Counterparty / credit]
    - Concentration of Credit Risk The counterparties to the Company’s forward contracts consist of a number of investment grade major international financial institutions.
    - The Company could be exposed to losses in the event of nonperformance by the counterparties.
    - However, the credit ratings and the concentration of risk in these financial institutions are monitored on a continuous basis and present no significant credit risk to the Company.
    - The applicable margin ranges from 0.875% to 1.375% depending on our credit ratings.

  [Valuation]
    - Neither the fair values of currency derivatives, which are determined based on a pricing model that uses spot rates and forward prices from actively quoted currency markets that are readily observable (Level 2 inputs under the fair value hierarchy described in Note 12), nor the balances included ...
    - The sensitivity analysis is a measurement of the potential loss in fair value based on a percentage change in exchange rates.
    - Based on the firmly committed currency derivative instruments in place at December 31, 2025, a hypothetical change in fair value of those derivative instruments assuming a 10% adverse change in exchange rates would result in an additional unrealized loss of approximately $4.3 million.
    - This amount, when realized, would be partially offset by changes in the fair value of the underlying transactions.
