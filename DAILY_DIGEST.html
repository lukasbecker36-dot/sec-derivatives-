Leads

DHR — Danaher's 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-09-25 reiterates that it uses cross-currency swap contracts to partially hedge net investments in non-U.S. operations in currencies including the Danish kroner and Japanese yen, and separately discloses a second, specific use: "The Company also uses cross-currency swap derivative contracts to hedge U.S. dollar-denominated long-term debt issuances in a foreign subsidiary whose functional currency is the euro against adverse movements in exchange rates."

ISRG — Intuitive Surgical's 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-09-30 states: "The amounts reclassified to revenue and expenses related to the hedged transactions and the ineffective portions of cash flow hedges were not material for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, and 2025." The same note goes on to discuss the company's use of derivatives not designated as hedging instruments.

JNJ — Johnson & Johnson's 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-09-27 restates its hedge-accounting policy: "Changes in the fair value of derivatives are recorded each period in current earnings or other comprehensive income, depending on whether the derivative is designated as part of a hedge transaction, and if so, the type of hedge transaction," with amounts in AOCI "reclassified to earnings in the same account as the hedged transaction." The filing also specifies a cap on hedge duration: "The maximum length of time over which the Company is hedging transaction exposure is 18 months, excluding interest rate contracts and net investment hedge contracts."

CSX — CSX's 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-09-30 ties an existing interest-rate swap position to a specific planned refinancing: "These swaps were effected to hedge the benchmark interest rate associated with future interest payments related to the anticipated refinancing of $850 million of 3.25% notes due in 2027, which is expected to occur no later than 36 months following the maturity of those 2027 notes." The filing separately describes the accounting treatment: "Gains and losses resulting from changes in fair value of the interest rate swaps offset changes in the fair value of the hedged portion of the underlying debt with no gain or loss recognized due to hedge ineffectiveness."

T — AT&T's 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-09-30 discloses: "We have fixed-to-fixed and floating-to-fixed cross-currency swaps on foreign currency-denominated debt instruments with a U.S. dollar notional value of $41,144 [million] to hedge our exposure to changes in foreign currency exchange rates and interest rates."

WAB — Wabtec's 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-09-30 describes a hedging use tied to intercompany financing: "these exposures using foreign currency swap contracts and cross-currency swaps to offset the potential income statement effects on intercompany loans denominated in non-functional currencies." The same filing discloses recent acquisition-linked FX hedging — "foreign exchange forward contracts with a notional value of €690 million" for the Frauscher acquisition (Q4 2025) and "€600 million" for Dellner Couplers (Q1 2026). Against that backdrop, Wabtec's designated FX notional rose to $729 million as of September 30, 2026 from $478 million as of March 31, 2026, a 52.5% increase.

NFLX — Netflix's 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-09-30 shows a larger FX earnings exposure: "If an adverse change in exchange rates of 10% was applied to our monetary assets and liabilities denominated in currencies other than the functional currencies as of September 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025, income before income taxes would have been approximately $76 million and $1 million lower..." — the $76 million figure is up from $55 million as of June 30, 2026, per the prior quarter's extraction. The filing also discloses an expanded interest-rate swap book: "As of September 30, 2026, we have entered into interest rate swap agreements with an aggregate notional amount of $2,400 million, which we designated as fair value hedges of specifically identified tranches of our fixed-rate Senior Notes," up from $1,400 million as of June 30, 2026 (+71.4%), with the associated 100bp fair-value sensitivity rising in step to $24 million from $14 million.

URI — United Rentals' 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-09-30 reports foreign revenue of $1,150 million, up from $361 million as of March 31, 2026; the filing frames its overall FX stance by saying "Based on the size of our foreign operations relative to the Company as a whole, we do not believe that a 10 percent change in exchange rates would have a material impact on our earnings." Separately, the filing states "A one percentage point decrease in market interest rates as of September 30, 2026 would increase the fair value of our fixed rate indebtedness by approximately 3 percent," down from a 10.2 percent sensitivity as of June 30, 2026.
