Kimberly-Clark (KMB) — 10-Q, period ended 2026-09-30. The filing discloses that hedge accounting was dropped on a sizeable foreign-exchange and commodity book: "For the nine months ended September 30, 2025, we discontinued cash flow hedge accounting for certain foreign exchange and commodity instruments with a notional value of $694 [million] because the forecasted transactions were no longer probable of occurring due to the IFP Transaction." The filing also flags a mechanical change to hedge-effectiveness testing: "We exclude the interest accruals on cross-currency swap contracts and the forward points on foreign exchange forward contracts from the assessment and measurement of hedge effectiveness."
Cardinal Health (CAH) — 10-Q, period ended 2026-09-30. Cardinal Health closed out a cross-currency swap book and immediately re-entered a similar position a year earlier: "In September 2026, we terminated the ¥18 billion ($120 million) cross-currency swaps entered into in September 2025 and received settlement in cash of $6 million," following a prior cycle in which "in September 2025, we terminated the ¥18 billion ($120 million) cross-currency swaps entered into in September 2023 and received settlement in cash of $3 million." The filing confirms these instruments "are designated as hedges of net investments."
Expedia (EXPE) — 10-Q, period ended 2026-09-30. Expedia reports a new hedging programme: "In the first quarter of 2026, we initiated a foreign exchange cash flow hedging program to minimize the effects of foreign currency fluctuations on a sub-set of future revenue." Separately, the filing notes that "in August 2025, the 2022 swaps were effectively closed out by entering into a swap with offsetting terms, and we de-designated the 2022 swaps and discontinued hedge accounting." The notional designated as a hedging instrument fell to $411 million at September 30, 2026 from $960 million at June 30, 2026.
